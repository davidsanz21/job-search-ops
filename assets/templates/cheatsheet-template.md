# Cheatsheet template

> Markdown conventions for `--style cheatsheet`. The renderer styles these patterns directly: H1 = title, the first blockquote = framing box, H2 = section bands, blockquotes inside sections = prepared answers, lines starting `▸` = delivery notes.

```markdown
# {Company} — {Interview type}
{Role} · {format, duration} · {interviewer name & title if known}

> **FRAMING:** {nature of the call in one line}. **Objective:** {what to walk away with}.
> **Angles, in order:** (1) {…} · (2) {…} · (3) {…}
> **Hard rules:** {never say X} · {never do Y}

## 1 · {First key question or theme}
> "{The crafted answer. **Bold** the load-bearing phrases the user should hit even if everything else drifts.}"

▸ {Delivery note: pacing, where to pause, what to end on, what never to say here.}

## 2 · {Next question}
> "{Answer…}"

▸ {Delivery note.}

## Power phrases — one at a time, then let it breathe
- "{Phrase.}" — **USE:** {the moment it's for}
- "{Phrase.}" — **USE:** {…}

## Your questions for them (ranked)
1. "{The alliance/process question that matters most}"
2. "{…}"

## The one concrete ask to land before hanging up
> "{The exact sentence that converts goodwill into a next step.}"

**Tone:** {one line — e.g., calm = senior; concede once without apologizing twice; you belong in this conversation.}
```

**Length discipline:** recruiter screen ≤2 pages · hiring-manager round ≤4 · final/panel ≤6–7. Cut questions before cutting delivery notes — a sheet the user can't scan mid-call is worthless.
